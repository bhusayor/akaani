Section 02 of 06

Your plate & portion guidance

  • 50% Vegetables & soup
  • 25% Protein
  • 25% Swallow or rice

One plate, three parts. It works whether you are eating rice, swallow or beans.

Section 03 of 06

Your nutrition priorities

  • Anchor breakfast with a protein, not just bread or pap.
  • Cut frying to twice a week. Boil, grill or bake the rest.
  • Bring vegetables into the two meals you cook most often.
  • Move your heaviest swallow to earlier in the day.

Few enough to actually do. Nobody changes ten things at once.

Section 04 of 06

Foods & meals to prioritize

  • Efo riro
  • Moi moi
  • Ofada rice
  • Beans & plantain
  • Okra soup
  • Mackerel
  • Garden egg
  • Unripe plantain

Foods you can buy at your usual market, not imported substitutes.

Section 05 of 06

Practical Nigerian food swaps

  • White rice→Ofada or brown rice
  • Fried plantain→Boiled or grilled plantain
  • Soft drink→Zobo, no sugar
  • Buttered bread→Bread with eggs or beans

Same meal, small change. Nothing on this list is a food you have to give up.

Section 06 of 06

Sample meal combinations

  • BreakfastPap with moi moi, and a boiled egg.
  • LunchOfada rice, efo riro, grilled fish.
  • DinnerBeans and unripe plantain porridge.
  • If hungryGroundnuts, garden egg, or an orange.

Mix, match and repeat across the week. These are starting points, not rules.

Your dietitian

Who am I talking to?

A Registered Dietitian. Not a nutritionist, not a coach.

RD is a protected title. Anyone can call themselves a nutritionist. Nobody can call themselves an RD without the training, the supervised practice and the licence behind it.

RDWhat that licence lets them do
  • 01Read your lab results and build your food around them.
  • 02Work with your medication, not against it.
  • 03Know what rice, swallow and soup actually do to your numbers.
  • 04Say when something needs your doctor, not a meal plan.

Everything else you are wondering

How is this different from advice online?

Advice online is written for everyone, so it fits nobody in particular. Your dietitian works from your readings, your medication, your budget and the food you actually cook. That is what you are paying for.

Will I have to stop eating Nigerian food?

No. Your guide is built on what you already cook and buy, adjusted in portion, pairing and frequency. Nothing gets swapped for salmon and quinoa.

What if I need to reschedule?
